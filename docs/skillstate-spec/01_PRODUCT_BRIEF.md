# Product Brief

## Product

**SkillState**

Tagline: **Plan. Learn. Prove. Grow.**

## Core problem

Learners know roughly where they want to go, but they struggle with four questions:

1. What does the destination actually require?
2. What do I already know well enough?
3. What am I missing or falsely confident about?
4. What should I do next, given my time, stage, and uncertainty?

Most learning products start with a course catalog. SkillState starts with the **end state** and works backward.

## Product model

SkillState maintains six states:

1. **Destination** — where the learner wants to go.
2. **Expected State** — capabilities normally needed by the learner's current stage.
3. **Claimed State** — what the learner says they know.
4. **Verified State** — what evidence supports.
5. **Gap State** — missing, weak, or unproven capabilities.
6. **Adaptive Journey** — the smallest credible path from current verified state to destination.

## Two primary entry modes

### A. Exploring / uncertain learner

Example:
- Class 12 student
- Interested in technology
- Unsure whether AI, data, backend, cybersecurity, design, etc.

Behavior:
- do not force specialization;
- generate related destinations;
- identify shared foundations;
- build a 12–24 month optionality-preserving plan;
- clearly show when a decision point is expected;
- explain which actions keep the most paths open.

### B. Exact destination learner

Example:
- 3rd-year undergraduate
- Wants to become an AI Engineer

Behavior:
- work backward from AI Engineer;
- infer what should already be known at this stage;
- ask the learner what they have learned;
- verify important claims;
- detect:
  - never-learned gaps,
  - weak skills,
  - false confidence,
  - knowledge without practical proof,
  - strong skill without portfolio evidence;
- create a personalized recovery/acceleration plan.

## Universal career support

The core must support careers outside engineering.

Examples:
- AI Engineer
- Data Engineer
- Backend Engineer
- Cybersecurity Analyst
- UX Designer
- Product Manager
- Financial Analyst
- Accountant
- Digital Marketer
- Management Consultant
- Content Strategist
- Research Analyst

The system must **generate destination requirements dynamically** instead of relying on hand-maintained curricula.

## The four readiness dimensions

A learner can be strong in one dimension and weak in another:

1. **Knowledge** — understands concepts.
2. **Proof** — has demonstrated ability through tasks/projects.
3. **Experience** — has used the capability in a realistic setting.
4. **Signal** — has visible evidence others can evaluate.

Do not collapse these into one fake precision score.

## Primary UX philosophy

Career planning is daunting. The interface must feel:
- calm,
- exploratory,
- hopeful,
- interactive,
- reversible.

It must never feel like:
- a giant spreadsheet,
- a corporate HR dashboard,
- a fantasy RPG,
- a course marketplace,
- a generic AI chat product.

## Signature interaction

**Path morphing.**

Changing a destination should visibly preserve already-verified foundations while changing future branches and priorities.

## Signature explanation

Every recommendation has a **Why this?** explanation.

Example:

> Learn Python fundamentals now because it supports 7 of your 8 currently plausible paths and unlocks future project work.

## Main product promise

**You do not need to know your entire future today. SkillState helps you take the next action that moves you forward without unnecessarily closing doors.**
